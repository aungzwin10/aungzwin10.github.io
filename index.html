About

I like the boring parts.

I started as a programmer in Mandalay in 2017, implementing electronic medical records in Myanmar hospitals. That meant on-site training and a lot of sitting with nurses, working out why the software everyone had signed off wasn't the software anyone could use. Going to the source instead of trusting the spec is the habit that got me promoted, and I've never given it up.

Since then I've built an attendance system for the Japanese market, an integrated database spanning South-East Asia, an ERP in Perl, and the Spring Boot APIs under a suite of clinical apps now running in more than forty hospitals. I've been an Application Development Manager since 2020, which for me means eleven products at once: I take the requirements myself, estimate and sequence them, decide the architecture, write a good deal of the backend, and read what the team ships before it reaches a ward. What I'm good at is holding all of that without dropping any of it.

8 years of this has taught me one thing above everything: correctness beats cleverness. A feature nobody can find is a feature that doesn't exist. A migration that half-works is worse than no migration. An estimate you can't defend is a promise you'll break. I'd rather ship the plain version that holds — and in healthcare, that preference isn't philosophical.
