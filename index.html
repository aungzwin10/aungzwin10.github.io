About

I like the boring parts.

I started as a programmer in Mandalay in 2017, implementing electronic medical records for Myanmar hospitals — which mostly meant sitting with nurses and finding out why the software everyone had specified wasn't the software anyone could use. That habit, of going to the source rather than trusting the spec, is the whole reason I ended up managing.

I've since built an attendance system for Japan, an integrated database for Southeast Asia, an ERP in Perl, and the Spring Boot APIs behind a suite of enterprise apps now running in more than forty hospitals across five countries. Today I run eleven products in parallel — I take the requirements myself, plan and estimate them, architect them, write a good deal of the backend, and review what the team ships. Healthcare is the domain my company works in; running things that can't be allowed to fail is the actual job.

Eight years of that has taught me one thing above everything: correctness beats cleverness. A feature nobody can find is a feature that doesn't exist. A migration that half-works is worse than no migration. An estimate you can't defend is a promise you'll break. I'd rather ship the plain version that holds.
